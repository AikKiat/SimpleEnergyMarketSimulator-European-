From my learning - a power plant **buys fuel** and **sells electricity**.

> "The spark spread is the theoretical gross margin of a gas-fired power plant from selling a unit of electricity, having bought the fuel required to produce this unit of electricity. All other costs (operation and maintenance, capital and other financial costs) must be covered from the spark spread." ("Spark spread," n.d.)

The same source gives the calculation as `SS = pE - pG/ηel`, where `ηel` is the plant's **electrical efficiency**, noting that "Typically, an efficiency of 50% is considered for gas-fired plants."

    ```
    pE: Price of electricity in MU/MWh
    pG: Price of natural gas in MU/MWh or MU/Btu
    ηel: Electrical efficiency resp.
    ```

I also found that the same idea carries different names depending on the fuel: The terms **dark spread**, **quark spread** and **bark spread** refer to different fossil fuels - coal, nuclear and bio-mass. ("Spark spread," n.d.)

Therefore, when we factor all other costs and abstract the context down to the **marginal costs** of obtaining gas vs the marginal cost of electricity produced, we can calculate the **spark spread** which illustrates the **profitability** of running a plant at a given moment.

In the instance of the **Day-Ahead Market**, the auction would produce **24 hourly prices** for the next day, and this determines the hourly schedule of power plants (whether they should run or not, at least in this very simplified scenario)
