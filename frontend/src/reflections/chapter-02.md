There are never cases of 100% **efficiency** since a % of energy is always converted to **waste** such as heat.

Therefore, a more **efficient** plant naturally has an edge over others in reaping **better returns** per unit of electricity produced, and thus is able to be **switched on** in place of other less efficient counterparts.

However, the conventional use of power plants relying on **fossil fuels** is never truly sustainable for the long road ahead, and **Green energy** is much more fundamental to developing a **sustainable world**. This is even more fortuitous when old powerplants can be **repurposed**, and "existing infrastructure can be reused in a climate-friendly way" (RWE, n.d.).

From the same article citing notable acts of impactful, economically viable ways of **infrastructure transformation**:

> "In Lingen, Lower Saxony...fossil-only site (has become)..seedbed of new energy technology (via)...two **hydrogen electrolysis** plants..., (with) The smaller 14 MW pilot plant ...in operation since August 2024...produc(ing) up to 270 kilograms of **green hydrogen** per hour. (Hence, we can see that)...fossil fuel infrastructure is being repurposed for new energy solutions – **completely CO2-free**." (RWE, n.d.)

The article adds that "The larger plant is currently under construction and is expected to supply green hydrogen with a generation capacity of **300 MW** in the medium term." (RWE, n.d.)
