> "The merit order is a way of ranking available sources of energy, especially electrical generation, based on ascending order of price (which may reflect the order of their short-run marginal costs of production) and sometimes pollution, together with amount of energy that will be generated. In a centralized management scheme, the ranking (of precedence is based on) lowest marginal costs. (These are first) brought online to meet demand, and the plants with the highest marginal costs are the last to be brought on line. Dispatching power generation in this way, known as economic dispatch, minimizes the cost of production of electricity." ("Merit order," n.d.)

The **marginal cost** does not account for fixed costs such as construction costs of a plant.

In the instance of the **Day Ahead Market**, a contractual demand is set at the ongoing auction, and based on **merit order**, power plants are considered in ascending order of price per electricity generated until the demand is met.

Next Kraftwerke frames the same idea operationally: "The merit order is based on the lowest marginal costs, i.e. the (operating) costs incurred by a power plant for the last megawatt hour produced." (Next Kraftwerke, n.d.)

Overall, the last, most expensive plant to run is the **marginal plant**. The price of this plant is also the **uniform price** for all other deployed plants before it in the merit order, and it thus represents the **market clearing price** in the case of it being the last accepted bid. More on this in my next finding related to the auction.

One more notable, interesting finding: the **Merit Order Effect (MOE)** is a case where the coming of renewable energies has caused conventional power plants to be continuously shifted further and further back down the line. During peak load periods, only the **residual load** would be served by conventional power plants. A concrete measure of this effect: a Fraunhofer Institute study found it "had allowed solar power to reduce the price of electricity on the German energy exchange by **10% on average**, and by as much as **40% in the early afternoon**." ("Merit order," n.d.)
