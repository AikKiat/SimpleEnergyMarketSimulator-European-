> "The merit order is a way of ranking available sources of energy, especially electrical generation, based on ascending order of price (which may reflect the order of their short-run marginal costs of production) and sometimes pollution, together with amount of energy that will be generated."
>
> "In a centralized management scheme, the ranking of precedence is based on the bidding offers by producers, influenced by marginal costs. These are brought online to meet demand, and the plants with the highest marginal costs are the last to be brought online. Dispatching power generation in this way, known as economic dispatch, minimizes the cost of production of electricity."
>
> ("Merit order," n.d.)

The **marginal cost** does not account for fixed costs such as construction costs of a plant.

In the **day-ahead market**, a demand quantity is submitted to the auction. Based on the **merit order**, power plants are considered in ascending order of their offers until demand is met.

Next Kraftwerke frames the same idea operationally: "The merit order is based on the lowest marginal costs, i.e. the (operating) costs incurred by a power plant for the last megawatt hour produced." (Next Kraftwerke, n.d.)

Overall, the last accepted plant is the **marginal plant**. Its offer becomes the **uniform market-clearing price** for all accepted plants in a pay-as-clear auction. More on this in my next finding related to the auction.

One more notable, interesting finding: the **Merit Order Effect (MOE)** is a case where the coming of renewable energies has caused conventional power plants to be continuously shifted further and further back down the line. During peak load periods, only the **residual load** would be served by conventional power plants. A concrete measure of this effect: a Fraunhofer Institute study found it "had allowed solar power to reduce the price of electricity on the German energy exchange by **10% on average**, and by as much as **40% in the early afternoon**." ("Merit order," n.d.)

FlexPower makes the fuel-cost intuition clear:

> "On the power market, solar and wind farms have the lowest marginal costs because their primary energy sources are free. Generating an additional megawatt hour of electricity from conventional power plants, on the other hand, costs a certain amount of fuel such as coal, gas or oil. In addition, the producers have to purchase a corresponding amount of CO2 emission certificates."
>
> (FlexPower, n.d.)

As such, the Merit Order Effect is the situation where more costly power plants are pushed further along the energy generation stack and therefore have fewer opportunities to clear the auction. This gives precedence to lower-cost facilities, including renewables, and can lower electricity prices.
